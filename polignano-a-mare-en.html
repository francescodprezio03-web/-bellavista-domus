What to See in Polignano a Mare

Polignano a Mare is one of the most photographed towns in Italy: whitewashed houses perched on a cliff overhanging the Adriatic, narrow alleys that suddenly open onto panoramic terraces and, far below, turquoise water crashing against the rock.

The Old Town on the Cliffs

The heart of Polignano is its old town, built entirely on a limestone spur jutting out over the sea. Wandering through its whitewashed alleys, past archways, small squares and flower-decked balconies, offers sudden glimpses of the Adriatic at every turn: it's one of the reasons the town has become one of Puglia's symbolic landmarks, captured in thousands of photographs.

Lama Monachile

This is Polignano's most iconic sight: a small pebble beach tucked into a narrow inlet between two rock walls, overlooked by the Bourbon bridge that was once part of the ancient Via Traiana. It's also where the traditional cliff-diving competition takes place each summer, drawing athletes from the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series to Polignano.

The Sea Caves

Beneath the old town, the sea has carved numerous caves into the rock, some visible only from the water: boat trips and kayak tours let you explore them up close, amid turquoise light and small coves reachable only by sea. One of the most famous caves is now home to a restaurant carved into the rock, the celebrated Grotta Palazzese, with a direct view over the Adriatic.

A practical warning about the boat trips: prices vary a great deal and some offers are well above what they should be. If you like, write to us before booking: we will help you find the good deal of the moment.

In the Footsteps of Domenico Modugno

Polignano a Mare is the birthplace of singer-songwriter Domenico Modugno, author of "Nel blu dipinto di blu" (Volare). A statue dedicated to him looks out over the sea right above Lama Monachile, and has become one of the favourite stops for anyone visiting the town.

Where to Eat

In the old town you'll find bakeries, gelaterias and small venues serving the flavours of Puglia: focaccia, taralli, artisan gelato and fresh seafood dishes. Many places are set in old cellars or look straight out over the cliffs, for a meal with a sea view you're unlikely to forget.

When to go and where to park

The advice that matters most is about timing: early morning or after 7 pm. In the middle of the day in summer the old town is packed and looking for a parking space becomes a waste of time.

Municipal car parks cost roughly €2-3 per hour. From Torre a Mare it is about a twenty-minute drive.

If Lama Monachile is too crowded

Lama Monachile is small, and in high season you feel it. Early in the morning or in the late afternoon it stays enjoyable; otherwise there are equally beautiful, less busy coves nearby — Cala Paura is the one we suggest most often.

Somewhere to eat

Pescaria: fish street food, well known and reasonably priced. It is the easiest way to eat well in Polignano without ending up in a place built for passing tourists.

Nearby

Polignano a Mare sits on the coast south of Bari, about twenty minutes' drive from Torre a Mare, and has its own stop on the Bari–Lecce railway line. Continuing along the coast you soon reach Monopoli, with its historic harbour, while inland lie the trulli of Alberobello, a UNESCO World Heritage site.
