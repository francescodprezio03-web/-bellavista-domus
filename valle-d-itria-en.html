Nearby · Valle d'Itria

The Valle d'Itria

If Alberobello feels too crowded, the answer is a few kilometres away: the Valle d'Itria is a plateau of dry-stone walls, trulli scattered among vineyards and white towns visible from far off. They lie close together, and three fit into one day.

Locorotondo

Named after its circular plan: the old town is a ring of white houses with pitched roofs, the cummerse, found nowhere else. It is small and takes an hour to walk, but it may be the most photogenic of the valley. From the edges of the town the view drops away over the vineyards.

Cisternino

The most informal of the four. It is known for butchers' shops that turn into grill houses in the evening: you choose your meat at the counter and sit down to eat it there. It is the place for an unceremonious dinner after a day of driving around.

Martina Franca

The most elegant, with a baroque old town of palaces, arches and wrought-iron balconies. It has a different feel from the others: less rural, more urban. In summer it hosts a well-regarded opera festival.

Ostuni

The white city, visible for miles, perched on three hills with the sea on the horizon. It is the most touristy of the four and the largest, a maze of lime-washed alleys climbing to the cathedral. It is also the furthest from Torre a Mare, a little over an hour.

Planning the day

The towns are only a few kilometres apart, so you need a car but you drive very little. Three in one day is the sensible maximum: more turns the day into a collection of car parks.

As with Alberobello and Monopoli, after 7 pm is the best time: the coaches have left, the white stone takes the warm light and the towns go back to their residents. Cisternino at that hour is in its element.

From the countryside to the sea

An hour on the road and you are ten metres from the beach again.
