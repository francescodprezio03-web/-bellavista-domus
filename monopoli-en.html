What to See in Monopoli

Monopoli combines a baroque old town, a working fishing harbour and sandy beaches, about 35 minutes from Torre a Mare.

The Old Town and the Harbour

The old town looks straight out onto the old harbour, still a reference point for local fishermen today: colourful boats moored along the quay and nets left out to dry frame one of the city's most evocative corners. Walking here at sunset, through the old town's alleys and along the harbour front, is not to be missed.

Castle of Charles V

This 16th-century fortress overlooks the harbour and the walls that once protected the city from the sea. Today the castle hosts exhibitions and cultural events, and offers one of the best vantage points to admire Monopoli from the water.

The Beaches

Unlike Polignano, Monopoli also has wide stretches of sandy beach, ideal for families with children looking for something easier than the cliffs and rocky coves further up the coast.

Our favourite is Cala Incine, a mix of pebbles and sand, free to access. It takes a bit of effort to reach, but it is worth it. Also free and more sheltered, the Grotta della Cala Tre Buchi.

If you would rather have a sunbed, an umbrella and a bar, Cala Paradiso and Lido Sabbiadoro are the ones we recommend.

Where to eat in Monopoli

Local cooking. Trattoria La Locanda dei Mercanti or La Locanda sul Porto, roughly €20-40 per person.

Fish. Trattoria Il Brigante, mainly fish, around €30-50 per person.

Meat. Garibaldi Pub & Steakhouse or Vecchie Storie Macelleria Braceria, €20-40 per person.

Prices are indicative and change with the season.

Cathedral of Madonna della Madia

The city's baroque cathedral houses the venerated Byzantine icon of the Madonna della Madia, patron saint of Monopoli, and is a place of devotion and one of the old town's landmark sights.

Where and when to park

There are public car parks in town. The difficult hours are lunchtime, dinnertime and mid-afternoon: outside those windows you will find a space without circling for long.

At the beach it is easier: there is free parking near the public beaches, while the lidos have their own paid car park.

Getting There

About 35 minutes by car or by train from Torre a Mare, always along the Adriatic line.
