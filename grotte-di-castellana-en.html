The Castellana Caves

About forty minutes by car from Torre a Mare lies one of the most spectacular cave systems in Europe: sixty metres below the surface, three kilometres of galleries carved out of the limestone by water.

Two routes, two different visits

Visits are guided only, and you choose the route when you buy the ticket. This matters: the two itineraries are genuinely different experiences.

The short route covers about one kilometre in 50 minutes and stops at the Caverna del Precipizio. The full route runs three kilometres in 100 minutes and is the only one that reaches the White Cave.

The White Cave

It is the reason to choose the longer route: a chamber of brilliant white alabaster, considered among the most luminous in the world. Those who take the short route do not see it.

What to bring, even in August

Underground the temperature stays around 16 degrees all year round. In August you step out of thirty-degree heat into cool, damp air: bring a jumper, however absurd it feels as you put it in the car. Closed shoes help too — there are steps and the floor can be slippery.

Before you set off

Opening times and availability change with the season, and in peak periods tours sell out: it is worth checking and booking on the official site before travelling. Tickets are for a specific time slot, not open entry.

Castellana Grotte pairs well with Alberobello or a tour of the Valle d'Itria: they all lie in the same direction, inland to the south of Bari.
